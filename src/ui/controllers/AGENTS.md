# UI controller contract

## Purpose

Own route-local UI state and temporary dynamic control construction behind typed detached views.

## Local Contracts

- Controllers receive an explicit target container and detached values; they never receive `GameSession`, repositories, or the owning router.
- Controllers emit typed intents, host actions, or presentation-setting changes. They never mutate gameplay state.
- Controller scripts reached by the background application graph must not script-preload imported textures or themes; resolve those resources only while constructing controller-owned controls on the main thread.
- `ScreenNavigator` alone mounts primary workspaces and restores route focus.
- `ScreenContentPresenter` owns route-local controller composition, detached route state, content rendering, and route-specific audio. The router supplies the mounted route body and navigation-owned back label; it does not render domain content.
- A controller may preserve selection, filtering, sorting, tabs, and draft text for its own route only.
- Application-route controllers own compact task-specific character selection inside their routed workspace. They do not depend on roster-row interaction, and item-local operations such as Trade keep their exact source item and recipient choices together.
- `InventoryScreenController` binds the scene-authored paper-white carried-item ledger, selected-character command rail, party selector, selected-item record, empty state, confirmation stage, and complete two-pack Trade workspace. It constructs no Controls: request-sized party members, items, facts, and Trade portraits instantiate only component scenes exported by their owning parent scene. Exact CICNs retain an explicit display size; each ledger line may annotate the detached `damage-range` or identified `armor` fact in Castle's red/blue language without recalculating either value. Selecting an item or refreshing the same character's inventory preserves the ordinary ledger viewport; switching characters deliberately starts the new ledger at the top. The ordinary Done action sits inside the lower item record. The ordinary and Trade ledgers bind the shared Alt-hover item-detail pane to the same detached `ItemView`; the pane never becomes a second selection or mutation path. Encounter selection reuses the same `InventoryWorkspace`, filters only by the request's exact owner/instance catalog, replaces ordinary mutation actions with one typed `Use in encounter`, and returns the request-owned hidden Classic identity without revealing it. Trade never preselects a row: two independently selected packs flank the scene-authored portrait matrix and Money/Items/Done spine; only an exact-instance cross-ledger drop submits Trade.
- `InventoryItemText` formats detached item records for ledgers, details, and action confirmations. `InventoryViewQueries` owns pure character/item selection and request-supplied transfer-target lookup; neither retains route state or emits an intent.
- `ServicesScreenController` owns the ordinary Party Wealth route, not a catalog of hypothetical services. It constructs no Controls and binds the complete scene-authored `ServicesWorkspace`, instantiating only exported character, transfer, location-service, and action records. Its modern slate composition uses one compact full-width pool summary above an adventurer ledger and selected-character Exchange workspace; each region uses shared inset chrome, a slate-readable selected-row treatment, and the exact application CICNs for gold, gems, and jewelry instead of a bespoke black well or recreated symbols. Classic composition condenses character selection into Exchange. Pool, Share, denomination increments, load facts, and disabled reasons remain core-owned, while Done and character selection are presentation-owned.
- `SpellsScreenController` binds the complete scene-authored `SpellsWorkspace` and constructs no Controls. The route uses a persistent right sidebar wider than the ordinary roster—420 pixels canonically and 288 pixels in the optional Classic profile—from the menu to the window bottom while preserving the map and narrative footer to its left. Both profiles keep the exact gradient `Spell Level` vertical rail and seven compact power choices beside 21-pixel same-level rows, a bounded one-line selected description, and four-column facts. Its Encounter-selection adapter instantiates that same exported workspace, reuses its caster selector, level rail, filtered list, and record, omits ordinary power/route operations, and emits only the request-eligible exact caster and packed Classic spell identity. Cast and Make Scroll mount in the ordinary route's fixed context-action strip beside Back rather than inside either the list or the route scroll. `Fast Spells (1–0)` names Castle's ten character-owned number-key bindings rather than implying a spell-speed class; each exported row assigns immediately when its picker changes, exposes only a separate Clear action, and survives Back through the resulting typed session rerender. Scroll Case remains separate and renders detached Use and Discard actions for each occupied exported row; both emit the same typed scroll intent because the session owns the exact cast-versus-discard interaction. The ordinary record uses the shared native-slate target badge only for catalog-proven gradient donor lettering; unproven target types remain ordinary semantic facts, and a combat-queue CICN is never treated as the spell's menu identity. The controller may format supplied scaling, target, resistance, save, cost, and availability facts but cannot infer legal powers, targets, effects, or resource identities.
- `CreatureLibraryScreenController` owns read-only selection and binding for the scene-authored Allies and Bestiary workspace. It consumes detached `MonsterView` or `MonsterCatalogEntryView` records and exact media keys; it never constructs stable controls, combat state, changes ally membership, or treats current allies as the Bestiary denominator. It may instantiate only the exported creature-row scene and switch authored panes to compact orientation. Exact CICN keys use nearest-neighbor sampling; unavailable media stays visibly absent rather than substituting a generic creature.
- `MapsJournalScreenController` constructs no Controls. It binds the complete scene-authored `MapsNotesWorkspace`, instantiating only its exported location-note, player-map, and journal-entry rows. The three route-local workspaces remain selectable saved/current location notes with a restrained parchment editor, acquired player maps on one dominant tiled-slate cartographic stage, and a scrollable two-page parchment journal with a leather spine and dark ink. The map stage has no nested Selected Map wrapper: its gold selected title and compact zoom tools occupy a shared inset header at the upper-left and upper-right corners, the authored description is centered immediately beneath that header, and the drawn map begins at the top of the remaining scroll region on its tiled parchment mat. Saved-note selection renders only the supplied detached historical `MapView`, including its saved darkness intensity, while the editor remains bound to the current location. The controller preserves tab, selected-record, filter, zoom, and Wide/Compact state across rerenders, emits only the typed current-location note intent, and binds `PlayerMapPresenter` on `PlayerMapCartographicStage`; it never acquires a map, edits authored journal text, changes map truth, or invents thumbnails for missing media.
- `SystemScreenController` exposes the host-owned traveled-area preview and Auto Note toggles in Controls plus the default-on Classic exploration-distance policy in Display. All persist only through `PresentationSettings`; map preferences change rendering from detached visited facts and Auto Note selects the acknowledgement payload, without mutating simulation directly. Its Audio tab keeps master, effects, and music volumes separate, persists global music enablement and source-classified Reduced Sound, and opens the shell-owned playlist modal without owning playback. Controls also owns the fixed keyboard/mouse reference derived from the public input contract; it does not imply unsupported remapping.
- `SystemScreenController` constructs no Controls and binds the complete scene-authored `SystemWorkspace`, instantiating only its exported detached save-slot row. Save & Load remains separate from Display, Audio, Pacing, Accessibility, Controls, and Diagnostics. Every preference tab uses a backed, vertically scrollable slate card so 800×600 at maximum text scale remains reachable; scene-authored rows align wide and stack in the compact `UiLayoutProfile`. Display pick lists use the shared Rebuilt-font option role in either typography mode. The Game menu exposes the full Save & Load route, and that workspace owns slot/source selection, one detached preview, and fixed host actions: Quick Save 1 retains legacy slot `quick`, Quick Save 2 uses `quick-2`, Save Selected overwrites the selected slot through the existing host boundary, and Save New Slot accepts only the repository's portable slot alphabet. A host-requested Save and Quit mode replaces ordinary save controls with one selected-slot Save and Quit action, while Back cancels that host mode. It distinguishes primary and backup loads and leaves corrupt/incompatible records visible with their exact reason. Preference tabs emit only existing typed presentation-setting changes and apply immediately; they never alter Classic rules or invent Apply/Reset, deletion, screenshots, schema mutation, or migration controls absent from the host contracts.
- `CampaignLibraryController` binds `front_door_menu.tscn` and `campaign_selection_panel.tscn`; it constructs no Controls. Those scenes own the Black Chancery identity, licensed looping application animation, separately looping click-enabled soundtrack, readiness-gated new/saved-adventure and Character Files actions, empty and selected-scenario states, and fixed package-operation host. Variable ready campaigns instantiate only the selector's exported `campaign_package_row.tscn`. The startup gate disables its three graph-dependent actions with one explanation and restores their original tooltips/focus atomically; Quit stays available. The controller separates one-time intro preparation, active playback, temporary suspension, and final resource release: the startup host may start the retained OGV behind its opaque card, ordinary route coverage never reopens it, and only final teardown releases it. The embedded video audio is never enabled, and the scene-authored independent MP3 player remains demand-loaded. It may resolve only the selected summary's exact `splashAssetId`; it cannot decode package content during discovery, classify free-form readiness errors, or retain terminal host failures absent from its detached operation view. External installation starts from one host file picker filtered to `.realmz2`; no developer path field appears in the ordinary library. Package progress disables competing install/refresh/scenario actions without displacing party controls. `CampaignPartySetupController` composes explicit shared setup state with independent inspection, party-assembly, and five-step creation controllers; its Load Saved Adventure action emits only a presentation route request to the package-bound System workspace. Those collaborators never inherit behavior from one another or receive the router. The facade preserves construction, visibility, focus, and its public signal/method boundary. Both public controllers mount under an explicit overlay host supplied by the shell scene.
- `PartySetupAssemblyController` owns one retained six-slot party-control set and updates its detached character, portrait, and availability values in place. Character-file rows may cache a prepared translucent drag texture keyed by revision and portrait identity; Add and drop continue to submit the same stable IDs, and neither gesture reads image pixels or uploads a texture on its activation frame.
- `CharacterScreenController` binds the scene-authored party-order summary, reorder actions, persistent shared sheet, no-character state, and complete Character Files list/history/inspection workspace. It may instantiate only exported party-order, vault-card, and history-row scenes for detached records. Repeated presentation must replace stable button signal bindings rather than accumulating callbacks; the controller never constructs stable Controls.
- `PartySetupCharacterCreationController` owns the full-stage five-step local draft surface. `PartySetupAppearanceEditor` owns the Appearance step's paging, thumbnail composition, proven portrait-to-tactical-icon pairing, and preview refresh. Identity, Race/Caste, Appearance, Review, and Spells may retain only presentation-local selections before emitting typed intents. Race drives the left/right selector relationship; each Race and Caste record pairs a narrow pressed selector with a wider detached detail, named-compatibility pane, and source-backed rule facts. Empty source descriptions occupy no UI and never become invented fallback prose. The controller may suppress only literal numbered fallback labels and may sort compatible Castes before incompatible records, but it cannot infer compiler provenance or calculate modifiers. Appearance exposes exact package portrait/tactical catalogs as paged two-row, six-column grids labelled from explicit `recommended_race_ids`; it never infers grouping from labels or numeric ranges. Portrait selection may follow the proven Classic resource pairing until the player independently overrides the tactical icon. Review consumes the detached provisional `CharacterView` through the shared complete character sheet; it may resolve that view's exact appearance and item media, but cannot calculate rules, invent restrictions, or substitute resource identities. Starting Spells shares `ClassicSpellSelectionChrome` with the ordinary spellbook, keeps only presentation-local level/focus state, and emits the complete typed selected-ID set so choices on hidden levels survive filtering. Pressed state communicates selection without modifier-key helper copy. `CharacterSpellOptionView` supplies the exact Classic resolution-effect resource sequence; the creator may animate only those IDs through scenario-first application-media resolution and must not derive art from a numeric spell ID. Actions remain outside the scrolling record body.

## Verification

- Exercise controllers through their public `present` and signal boundaries.
- Route lifecycle tests own primary-workspace exclusivity and modal input ownership; do not add one test per overlap symptom.
