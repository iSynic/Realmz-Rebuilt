# Purpose

- Own typed, focusable presentation components for serializable `InteractionRequest` kinds.

# Ownership

- Components translate an existing request payload into controls and emit the exact response payload selected by the player.
- `TextChoiceInteraction` renders yes/no as one backed compact response row and authored indexed/scenario options as numbered semantic choices using the dedicated Classic choice style. The shared presenter invents no generic title for these narrative requests. Positive acknowledgement has no formal button: clicking the narrative surface or pressing Enter/Space emits its one typed response. Journal-eligible acknowledgements never add a separate Take Note button: default-on Auto Note makes that narrative acknowledgement emit `{ "takeNote": true }`, while disabling it exposes Castle's proven `N` key to record and advance through the same typed acknowledgement. An already-recorded message exposes no duplicate mutation.
- `EncounterInteraction` preserves one backed stable original-art Action, Items, Skills, Speak, Spells, and Stop strip above one backed contextual task pane in a compact locked floating modal. Choice, word, and item tasks remain inside that modal; Spells temporarily replaces the right Party roster with its list/record/actions so the map, narrative footer, and persistent Encounter command strip remain visible. Speak uses the established Theldrow input/action treatment, bounds its word-or-phrase entry to 39 characters so Castle's 40-byte destination remains terminable, and exposes Castle's one proven submit path. Escape first cancels an open item/spell catalog, closing the roster-side spell workspace when present, and otherwise emits only an authored Back/Stop response; it cannot invent an unavailable exit. Unavailable commands remain disabled in place with an exact reason, and every submission retains the existing typed complex-encounter body. Castle's Scroll-versus-Spells/Stop control mapping remains unresolved evidence and must not be guessed through presentation remapping.
- `InteractionPresenter` owns request identity, modal visibility, and construction of `InteractionResponse`.
- `SelectionInteraction` keeps the active character prompt in the Classic textbox while `ClassicPartyRoster` owns the numbered portrait interaction; it must not recreate a checklist or a separate Choose button. Ally body-count selection remains in its dedicated list.
- `LifecycleInteraction` renders only the host-supplied End Adventure or process-Quit operations in a compact locked floating modal. Process Quit shows one question and one centered content-width Save and Quit / Quit / Cancel row without duplicate headings or explanatory prose; End Adventure may retain one short consequence line. It owns stable fixed actions, restrained destructive emphasis, and Escape routing to a declared Cancel action, then emits one declared action identity. It does not save, close a session, infer combat state, or reuse scenario-choice semantics.
- `AgeUpdateInteraction` presents exact typed portrait/tactical media, source-backed band/range, and nonzero fifteen-column age changes in one contained inset and emits only the empty `age_update` acknowledgement payload.
- `PlayerMapInteraction` owns the negative-opcode immediate display stage. It renders the detached player-map payload through the same presenter used by Maps/Notes and emits only the empty acknowledgement for the matching request ID; closing it cannot mutate acquisition state or bypass the issuing VM frame.
- `ThiefEncounterInteraction` owns the dedicated character-and-eight-action workspace supplied by the typed request. `PickLockInteraction` renders exact character identity, chance and countdown above its deterministic tumbler frames at the supplied cadence; the red, gold, and green track zones mirror Castle's thresholds, the final source second is held, and only the selected frame index is emitted. Neither component rolls RNG, recomputes ability values, mutates encounter flags, or invents a cancel outcome absent from Castle's picker.
- Encounter item and spell modes replace generic dropdowns with one domain-labelled list, selected record, and fixed confirm/cancel row; items are modal-local while spells mount in the presenter-owned roster rectangle. Encounter spells expose the shared exact gradient `Spell Level` heading and level-colored `Level #` rail derived only from their supplied Classic spell IDs, then filter the eligible list without changing the submitted record identity. `EncounterCatalogEntry` does not carry a proven item CICN or spell fact record, so presentation keeps the exact command-strip art and must not infer entry art from the Classic record number. Thief selection uses separate portrait-led character and source-valued action panes; disabled reasons come only from the request.
- `TreasureDistributionInteraction` owns `ordinary` and `completion-confirmation`. Ordinary mode renders Castle's single ordered booty queue—including leading fumbled items—in a responsive white 50×60-cell field (six columns in the compact profile and as many whole cells as the wide field permits), with a transient source-cell hover oval, exact item art, a typed item inspector, and a separately selected portrait-led recipient roster showing item count, movement, and load. Pool, Share, exact denomination transfers, Detect/Identify, and Done remain visible in the same workspace at 1280×720. Detect/Identify caster selectors use the established Theldrow picker role, while only an unidentified item's name receives Castle's source-backed hollow outline treatment. Clicking an eligible item emits one exact-instance assignment; after the session commits it, presentation alone carries a transient selection marker to the recipient and plays sound 6002 without withholding the resulting request/view, so another item may be assigned immediately. The animation never mutates, advances, or gates simulation. `fumbled-item-recovery` is retained only as a save-v4 visual adapter. Done retains the ordinary component across its response/rerender boundary; completion mode then explicitly confirms or cancels abandonment in a presenter-owned nested modal over that exact retained workspace.
- `ShopInteraction` owns one full-stage stock/pack/selected-item workspace with a fixed transaction footer. The compact profile uses a stock/pack browser beside the selected record rather than squeezing three columns. Shopper, stock, and carried-item selection are presentation-only; Buy, Sell, Identify, and Leave emit the exact typed payloads and preserve supplied availability reasons. Stock and carried-item records render only the request's exact typed resource key; presentation never infers an item CICN from a stable item ID or displays its numeric resource ID.
- `LevelUpInteraction` owns locked floating `result` and `spell-selection` modals. Result renders the committed level and four supplied gains with exact character portrait when the detached party view proves it. Spell selection uses the available application height rather than the roster height, reuses the exact gradient `Spell Level` heading, level-colored `Level #` parent filters, pressed Theldrow spell controls and inset record chrome, and renders the complete supplied candidate set, existing selections, exact point accounting, and exact spell CICNs only when the detached character spell view proves their resource identity. Switching levels filters the visible list without discarding hidden selections. It acknowledges the exact character result or returns one set of stable spell IDs; it never rolls, applies gains, computes eligibility, invents missing before/after facts, or changes character state.
- `TempleInteraction` owns a full-stage three-pane temple workspace: exact portrait-led adventurer selection, all supplied source-priced services, and a selected character/service inspector with fixed Purchase/Pool/Share/Leave actions. The optional 800×600 profile replaces the adventurer pane with a compact selector. It may disable Purchase only from the supplied selected character's available-gold fact, shows at most the first five supplied conditions, and emits only stable character/service identity or the named wealth action. It does not invent service effects or reuse unproven art.
- `BankInteraction` owns a full-stage three-pane Bank-backed Swap / pooled-departure workspace with separate account, adventurer, and selected-character transfer panes; the optional 800×600 profile replaces the adventurer pane with a compact selector. It renders only supplied pooled, banked, personal, load, Pool/Share, exact-increment, selection, and unavailable-reason facts. It emits Pool, Share, one stable character/denomination transfer, or Done. It never performs bank import/return, clears a pool, resumes movement, recomputes capacity, invents arbitrary deposit/withdraw controls, or turns Done into a deposit.
- `BattleInteraction` consumes the request's weapon mode, rules-owned melee/projectile targets, legal-action list, typed Escape availability, staged spell/power/cost choices, and source-probed charged-item/power/target combinations. Cast and item responses carry only actor, action, and stable item/spell/target identities plus spell power or selected battlefield coordinate/rotation where supplied. Automatic groups use an empty target ID plus the core-provided group label and omit individual HP. Memorized-spell targeting may select any request-provided living battlefield footprint or center, previews only the supplied Data AD offsets, and leaves final actor/range/LOS/mask legality to the core response transaction. Charged-item and scroll targeting retains its pre-probed candidates. The component never recalculates spell or item legality, masks, occupants, range, LOS, charges, or effects. Disabled Escape, Fire, spell, and item actions use typed reasons and cannot synthesize targets or tactical facts. Guard emits Castle's persistent defend command, while Finish is a separate response that clears Guard and remaining movement.
- `BattleInteraction` also consumes typed Auto Turn, Delay, Bandage, and Turn Undead probes. It emits the ordinary combat action plus at most one core-listed Bandage recipient; persistent character Auto is routed separately by the party roster and never masquerades as an activation command. Its fixed-height tactical record resolves exact active, initiative, and inspected combat icons through the supplied media map, collapses missing identities to text, and gives restrained emphasis only to Attack/Spells/Finish without changing command order or availability.
- `SelectionInteraction` keeps mandatory field-spell, scroll, and charged-item target selection in the Classic textbox/Party-roster composition. It renders only the request's strict spell-target context—spell identity, selected power, effective cost, icon key, and target facts—while the roster owns numbered target picks and emits the existing stable-ID response. It does not parse prompts, recalculate costs, or invent target art for unproven Classic target types.
- `SelectionInteraction` also owns a locked floating surviving-allies modal with supplied candidates, health, required selections, maximum capacity, and fixed Continue. It may join exact CICNs only from a matching detached `party_allies` stable ID, owns temporary selection and maximum enforcement, and emits only selected stable IDs. An empty request has a non-trapping fallback, but core/session flow remains responsible for skipping ally selection when no allies exist.
- The battle component is a full-window-width bottom command deck, not a full-stage modal. Its compact beveled overview keeps active and inspected facts beside a request-ordered initiative strip rotated to `NOW` and `NEXT`; presentation-supplied combat icons identify actors, with short names retained as the missing-icon fallback. Below that record, one centered Classic console groups natural-width controls as View, Action, and Tactics rather than stretching every label or inset across equal screen fractions. Each content-sized group retains two centered fixed rows beneath a larger centered and divided heading across actor and availability changes; semantic Classic color distinguishes viewing, action, and tactical commands without changing legality or order. Battle-specific theme margins keep the console inside the canonical 1280x720 combat region; unavailable commands stay in place with their supplied reason instead of making neighboring controls reflow. One target/spell/item workflow may replace that overview at a time and must provide a visible Back to battle action in both the canonical composition and optional 800x600 Classic mode. When battlefield targeting begins, that workflow hides its setup selectors and shows only Back, an explicit `Targeting` status, Confirm, and Cancel; cancellation restores the selectors. Spatial movement remains with `ClassicBattlefieldPresenter` and stays suppressed until the secondary workflow is closed or committed.
- Memorized combat spell selection replaces the persistent party rail and its inert lower command rectangle with one full-height Classic-shaped spellbook: the shared exact gradient `Spell Level` heading and level-colored `Level #` rail, unique level-filtered known-combat-spell list, legal power choices, supplied cost/target facts, and explicit Aim/Cast plus Back actions. Known spells without a supplied legal option remain inspectable and disable Cast with an explicit current-activation explanation; presentation never fabricates a `CastOption`. The bottom deck retains only workflow/targeting status and confirmation. The spellbook passes the exact selected `CastOption` back to `BattleInteraction`; it never reconstructs legality or submits directly to the session.

# Local Contracts

- Never add gameplay facts, targets, prices, eligibility, or service availability that are absent from the request.
- Disabled controls require a specific player-facing reason.
- Every pointer action must also be keyboard focusable.

# Work Guidance

- Keep request-kind logic in the narrowest component and preserve payload field names exactly.
- Repeated combat spells retain explicit addition order, permit one through `maximumTargets`, and emit that order as `targetIds`. Memorized spells use the request's living combatant roster for selection and defer final legality to submit; scrolls retain core-probed candidates. Presentation cannot reorder the selection or commit gameplay state.
- Battlefield target cancellation emits no response. Confirmation preserves the existing combat payload ABI: one stable `targetId`, ordered `targetIds`, or one legal `targetCoordinate` plus rotation, according to the configured mode.
