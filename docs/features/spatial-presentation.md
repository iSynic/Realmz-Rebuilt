# Spatial presentation

The map, battlefield, and first-person dungeon are algorithmic renderers surrounded by scene-authored controls. Their presenters may create rendering nodes and geometry where an authored scene cannot express variable topology, but they must consume detached authoritative views and retain their current incremental caches.

The 2D map, combat board, and 3D dungeon never invent movement, collision, LOS, target, or discovery rules. Their viewport, cameras, layers, materials, masks, and surrounding controls belong in scenes. `ClassicMapPresenter` owns 2D drawing and pointer input. `MapPresentationGeometry` owns viewport, discovery, LOS-mask, debug-outline, and pointer calculations, while `MapTextureCache` owns asset identities plus decoded atlas, overlay, darkness-mask, and land-marker reuse. `ClassicBattlefieldPresenter` draws the tactical stage and plays committed events; `BattlefieldInteractionController` owns tactical input, targeting, inspection focus, and presentation-only aids. `BattlefieldPresentationGeometry` owns combatant, camera, footprint, click-sector, and playback calculations, while `BattlefieldTextureCache` retains its decoded atlases, actors, and keyed effects. `DungeonSceneMeshBuilder` receives only `DungeonGeometryProjection`; it admits projected cells, appends stable surfaces and topology-owned boundaries, adds deduplicated corner columns, and materializes one shared mesh buffer. Ordinary movement therefore extends retained data and reuses media without rebuilding either layer. Use the rendered movement, dungeon-transition, combat, and navigation probes before and after a sensitive change.
