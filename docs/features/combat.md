# Combat

`RealmzContent.combat` exposes the immutable `CombatCatalog` for monsters, selectable monster sets, and battles. `CombatState` is the saved aggregate for the active battle, not a sixty-method utility object. Its battle identity, outcome, battlefield, and pending action/reaction sit beside five named state owners: `CombatRosterState` for monsters, `CombatTurnSequenceState` for initiative and Undo, `CombatActorStatusState` for Guard and battle-only character markers, `CombatDroppedItemState` for fumbled equipment, and `CombatSpellRuntimeState` for death-macro and persistent-field state. `CombatStateCodec` alone translates that composition to the unchanged flat save representation. Call the owner that holds the fact instead of adding a forwarding method to the aggregate; the aggregate itself coordinates only invariants that must update more than one owner, such as replacing or extending initiative and advancing a turn.

The battlefield follows the same ownership model. `BattlefieldState` keeps map identity, source placement, facing, and the two real collaborators. `BattlefieldTerrainState` owns the 90-by-90 terrain cells and navigation revision; `BattlefieldActorState` owns actor anchors, monster sizes, footprints, occupancy, movement, and swaps. `BattlefieldGrid` owns the shared dimensions and footprint geometry. `BattlefieldStateCodec` is the only flat save encoder/decoder, so rules call `battlefield.terrain` or `battlefield.actors` rather than passing through aggregate proxy methods. `BattlefieldRules.probe_path_step_toward_actors` reads as target admission, target-footprint collection, goal seeding, current-occupancy marking, then the retained bounded search.

`CombatFlow` is the stable command surface over battle setup, round, action, reaction, magic, field, navigation, and automation collaborators. `CombatBattleSetup` owns the complete transactional construction sequence before handing the committed battle to the lifecycle and automation owners. All collaborators share one explicit combat context and return typed results; callers must not reach into collaborator-private methods.

`CombatDecisionContext` shares admitted spell powers, occupancy, threat and targeting geometry only within one unchanged decision. `CombatSpellAdmission` retains legality and enumeration without constructing UI options. Forced emergency bandaging precedes pursuit; `CombatPursuitPlan` reuses only the initial monster step when terrain, occupancy, actor, target and movement still match and no contact reaction intervened. Category weights, targets, route ties and RNG draws remain unchanged. The application worker described in [ADR 0020](../adr/0020-exclusive-combat-session-worker.md) keeps these synchronous rules off the input thread; the simulation contains no scheduling or presentation timing.

Sight and ray queries retain the exact 128 floating-point sample advances while skipping repeated inspection of the same consecutive cell. Firing goals enumerate row bounds of the same floored-distance circle, preserving every admitted anchor and its ordering.

Inside the character-action owner, command admission, the command-specific mutation, interruption detection, battle completion, and automatic monster continuation are separate readable phases. Character and monster physical resolvers likewise prepare one typed attempt, roll physical and elemental damage in source order, apply authored specials, and then commit condition and health changes. Keep the ordering of weapon-condition, luck, hit, fumble, damage, special, and lifetime-record operations intact when changing these phases.

Player battle input begins at `CombatIntents`: `choose_action`, `move`, and `set_auto` construct the stable command kinds with `CombatIntentPayloads.Action`, `Move`, and `Auto`. They carry player choice only; `src/playthrough/combat/combat_command_workflow.gd` translates them to the rules-owned combat surface, where action admission and mutation remain in the combat collaborators below.

Battle confirmation, friendly collision, death macro, ally selection, fumble recovery, and reward handoffs live beside that command workflow under `src/playthrough/combat` and are created by `CombatContinuations`. `CombatContinuationBody` and `CombatRewardContinuationBody` retain only the facts needed to resume; `SessionContinuationCodec` preserves their established save representation.

Automated decisions enter through the deliberately small `CombatAiScoring` facade. Follow party Auto choices into `CombatPartyActionPlanner`, monster choices and spell plans into `CombatMonsterActionPlanner`, shared deterministic weighting into `CombatAiScoringSupport`, and read-only health, allegiance, immunity, and reflection questions into `CombatAiTargetFacts`. These planners consume the session RNG supplied by the combat transaction; they do not own randomness or presentation state. Monster target preference consumes one random slot draw, then immediately falls through to the stable visible-target scan when that slot is empty, friendly, unavailable, or obscured; a legal target never causes thousands of repeated empty-slot draws.

After a category is selected, `CombatFlowAutomation` routes it to `CombatPartyAutomation` for party Auto or `CombatMonsterAutomation` for monster phases and charmed turns. Monster advance retains the anchors settled during the current uninterrupted activation and supplies them to the deterministic route search; if its source-style fallback would revisit one, movement ends with an explicit unavailable reason instead of oscillating until its allowance is empty. `CombatOccupancyRules` is the one owner for hostility queries and defeated-actor battlefield cleanup. This keeps choice policy separate from the commands that mutate battle state.

`CombatProtectionThreats` is a deliberate Rebuilt AI improvement. Projectile shields, elemental protection, and level wards receive weight only when the current opposition or a persistent field supplies a matching threat. The scan reads actual spell, item, natural-attack, allegiance, and resource facts; unknown opponent death macros conservatively retain protection because their effects are not inferred. It does not require immediate range or assume a temporary casting restriction will last. Boolean wards value at most three rounds of duration, avoiding excess power bought solely for a longer duration. Other conditions retain their full value because their duration can also determine potency. These choices intentionally differ from earlier Auto behavior and can change subsequent RNG consumption; evaluating threats itself consumes no RNG. Manual casting and spell resolution remain unchanged.

Ordinary manual combat steps retain unaffected detached records while rebuilding the active character and combat facts. Reactions, fields, swaps, Auto, and unknown event sequences rebuild completely. Within each projection, Fast Spell choices and item-unavailability queries are shared rather than repeated for every binding or carried item. Persistent-Auto boundaries bind the retained activity strip without constructing a hidden manual command deck. Playback resolves actor names from detached base/final views before its first frame, retains native terrain pixels across animations, and redraws stationary frames only once. Default movement uses 80 milliseconds of interpolation plus a 20-millisecond arrival frame; Move To waits for playback and the drawn revision, then validates its next ordinary step without an extra exploration-repeat delay. These presentation timings are Rebuilt choices, not Classic gameplay rules.

Physical attacks enter through the equally small `CombatRules` facade. Character attacks and weapon effects live in `CombatCharacterAttackResolver`; monster attacks and authored attack specials live in `CombatMonsterAttackResolver`; common weapon-effect and monster-save calculations live in `CombatAttackResolutionSupport`; initiative lives in `CombatInitiativeRules`. `CombatAttackPolicy` remains the pure admission, modifier, fumble, and weapon-dice policy shared by those resolvers.

Character combat magic enters through `CombatFlowMagic`, which owns the learned-spell, charged-item, and scroll transactions after `CombatSpellSelection` admits them. Read each public operation as source admission followed by a target-family dispatch: summon and Phase have their own owners; repeated, area, group, ray, party, and single-actor paths have named probe or resolution helpers. `CombatCharacterSpellResolution` collects shared ray, group, and area targets and owns the common mutation-and-event commit for those sources. Keep source-specific payment, rollback, Scroll Case mutation, and target requirements in `CombatFlowMagic`; keep the ordered attack/movement cost, result events, defeated-actor cleanup, death macros, and turn advancement in the shared resolver. This separation must not change spell RNG tags or their order.

The battlefield is authoritative state, while `CombatView` is detached presentation data. The UI submits typed commands and targets and plays already-committed events. `ClassicBattlefieldPresenter` owns tactical drawing, while `CombatPlaybackController` turns the ordered committed event stream into a cosmetic timeline. Its outer pass owns battle/Auto cadence; named appenders own cues, movement, physical attacks, magic, status results, field lifecycle, and retreat while sharing event-local position, health, persistent-field, and hidden-combatant timelines. Damage appears before defeat removal, and terminal routes and music remain on the presented combat view until that sequence settles. Space skip discards unplayed cues, stops active combat sound and portrait effects, then binds the committed final view before the next activation. Default-off Condense Multi-Target Playback combines uninterrupted, distinct-target spell or indexed attack sequences into one animation. The retained combat account records every target result in source order, simultaneous target results precede grouped defeat cues and use the detached event facts, and special sounds or intervening actions keep ordinary playback. The existing Hurry setting identity remains in presentation preferences; combat state and RNG never change. `BattlefieldInteractionController` owns pointer and keyboard translation, targeting, inspection focus, Reveal Friends, and movement-cost aid state. `BattlefieldPresentationGeometry` owns stateless combatant, camera, footprint, click-sector, and playback calculations, while `BattlefieldTextureCache` retains decoded atlases, actors, and keyed effects. Begin rules work in `test_combat_flow.gd`, navigation work in `test_battlefield_navigation.gd`, and measure sensitive changes with both combat performance probes.

The scenario runtime's `CombatInteractionRequestBuilder` is the command-deck projection boundary. It assembles legal actions, movement choices, targets, inspection facts, spell/item/scroll choices, and transient `CombatView` data without mutating the battle. `ClassicBattleLifecycleOperations` consumes the resulting typed response and owns only the resumable battle transitions around the game combat commands.

The visible command deck begins at `src/ui/combat/battle_interaction.tscn`. The scene owns the complete turn summary, initiative mount, View/Action/Tactics groups, fixed command slots, attack, spell, scroll, item, bandage, and inspection modes. `battle_interaction.gd` binds only request-supplied availability and detached facts; `BattleCommandScaleController` reapplies responsive sizes and compact density to those authored controls without rebuilding them, and `battle_targeting_controls.tscn` supplies the reusable pointer-target confirmation mode. The 800×600 profile keeps both rows of every command group in its fixed footer, drops only duplicated/decorative initiative chrome, and disables the inapplicable outer interaction scrollbar. Spatial battlefield drawing remains algorithmic in `ClassicBattlefieldPresenter`, and the scene-authored command deck is retained and reflowed rather than rebuilt during a live resize. Presentation coverage lives in `tests/presentation/test_classic_ui_system.gd`.

Memorized combat spell selection begins at `src/ui/combat/combat_roster_spellbook.tscn`, embedded in the persistent Party rail. Its seven-level rail, bounded spell list, selected-spell identity, target badge, six rule facts, description and unavailable states, power strip, and fixed Cast/Back footer are directly editable. `CombatRosterSpellbook` instantiates only exported spell and power-button scenes and returns the exact request-owned `CastOption`; it owns no spell legality or combat state.
