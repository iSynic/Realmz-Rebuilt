# Realmz Rebuilt complete visual audit

This is the player-visible visual denominator for Realmz Rebuilt. Its 58 audited surfaces cover startup, all ten registered routes, all sixteen typed interaction families, creator/setup substates, combat/reward stages, and host failure states. It complements the workflow inventory: the inventory decides whether a workflow exists and is complete; this audit decides whether its presentation communicates the correct Realmz task clearly at the canonical `1280x720` composition and the optional `800x600` Classic composition.

Castle at `491816ad60037394f92c428e99c004494d3c28b3` is authoritative for workflow, information, command meaning, staging, and observable pacing. Remake at `86cf2bf391ef0c43ba31c1633ddd63b7e67e3d61` is a donor of modern Godot composition, decoded controls, and useful interaction patterns. Neither reference controls Realmz Rebuilt's typed ownership or responsive implementation.

## Audit decisions

- **Retain**: the dominant workspace and action hierarchy are sound; finish density, art, states, and compact reflow.
- **Refine**: the composition is recognizably Realmz but hierarchy, reachability, stability, or art use needs correction.
- **Recompose**: the current surface is a generic list/form or an overlay on the wrong workspace; rebuild it around the Castle task.
- **Hold**: do not invent a polished surface until Providence/core provides the missing player-visible facts.

Every screen must have one dominant workspace, stable primary actions, explicit loading/empty/error/unavailable states, and no overlap with a prior workspace. Original bitmap art is used only when its resource identity and license/provenance are established. PICT, CICN, portrait, tactical icon, item icon, spell target art, and integrated `snd ` media remain distinct typed resources.

## Shared visual-system findings

| Concern | Baseline finding | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global hierarchy | The slate/gold/cyan language is coherent, but generic headings and helper copy often compete with the actual task. | Classic menu, one dominant workspace, terse contextual facts, then commands. Remove explanatory copy that merely restates labels. |

| Original art | Exploration commands, portraits, tactical icons, item CICNs, scenario pictures, map tiles, battle terrain, and some spell/encounter controls are integrated; other surfaces still use text-only placeholders. | Prefer verified original art at integer scale where it conveys authored identity or a known command. | Command stability | Combat improved substantially, but some modal and service actions still move with list length or scroll out of reach. | Fixed footer/rail actions; disabled commands remain in place when Castle exposes the command but the current state forbids it. |
| Modal ownership | Earlier presentation defects allowed setup, inventory, or battle-return layers to coexist. The capture harness now reaches every route and typed interaction family; ordinary-play acceptance still owns real transition proof. | One primary route plus at most one blocking interaction layer. Back/Escape closes the top interaction first. |
| Compact mode | `800x600` sometimes scales a wide workspace instead of changing its information layout. | Deliberate 4:3 reflow with a square gameplay stage, condensed roster rows, and fixed reachable actions. |
| Accessibility | Focus treatment is visible, but text scaling can force controls below the viewport. | Reflow headings/facts; scroll record bodies only; preserve primary action and focus visibility at supported scale. |

These boards are screen-family composition contracts for all 58 audited surfaces, not a smaller replacement denominator. They establish pane ownership, hierarchy, fixed actions, original-art opportunities, and density. Closely related states reuse those contracts rather than receiving cosmetic variations. Additional compact boards are required only where the `800x600` information architecture materially differs.

Implementation proceeds in coherent batches:

1. Startup, scenario library, party assembly, Character Files, inspection, and all five creator steps.
2. Land/dungeon exploration, camp, scenario textbox/picture, choices, encounter commands, and typed pickers.
3. Character sheet, allies, inventory/equipment/trade, spellbook, and spell targeting.
4. Money, shop, temple, bank, treasure, fumble recovery, level-up, and ally selection.
5. Battle entry, tactical workspace, turn strip, commands, spellbook/targeting, playback, and terminal return.
6. Notes/maps, save/load, preferences, diagnostics, lifecycle, and failure surfaces.

Current remediation status:

- Batch 1 has recomposed the integrated scenario/Character Files/party assembly surface, Character Files route, setup inspection, and creator Appearance stage. The splash and remaining creator-stage refinements remain open in their rows.
- Batch 2 has recomposed the full-width exploration footer, fixed-measure narrative interaction stage, compact typed choices, party-roster character selection, and stable encounter command/picker strip. Camp-specific state, source-limited word entry, thief/item/spell picker refinements, and player maps remain open in their rows.
- Batch 3 now covers the full-width Character, Allies, Inventory, and field Spellbook workspaces plus the mandatory party-target stage. Character gives its sheet, six-member portrait selector, dense tabs, and source-backed equipment icons priority while keeping formation editing secondary. Allies owns one read-only current-companion list and record with exact CICN media, source-backed combat facts, a concise empty state, and a deliberate stacked Classic reflow. Inventory owns a compact character selector, dominant browser/record panes, fixed action dock, and item-local Trade recipients. Spellbook owns the Classic level/list/record/power hierarchy, scaled source facts, exact Cast/Abort and proven target art, two-column Fast Spell grid, five-slot scroll case, and fixed actions at both supported compositions. C09 now carries its authoritative caster/spell/power/cost/target display context through the strict saved interaction and uses the numbered Party roster rather than a generic picker; ordinary-play acceptance remains outstanding.

Repeated controls are components rather than independently styled mockups. Product implementation is complete only when its audit row reaches the acceptance contract below; a Pen board alone is not implementation or live evidence.

## Acceptance

- Every row above has a current capture or explicit missing-capture record, one Castle/Remake lead, a target decision, and a media decision.
- The fixture gallery captures all ten registered routes and every typed interaction family at `1280x720`; the layout-sensitive subset also captures `800x600`.
- No primary action, six-member roster, modal response, combat command, spell Cast/Back control, or reward completion action requires scrolling at the canonical resolution.
- A blocking interaction is opaque and visually owns the workspace; no prior route controls remain active or readable through it.
- Original art is used only by exact semantic/resource identity, at integer scale, with provenance in the owning catalog. Missing art is explicit and never substituted by a semantically different image.
- Ordinary AOGM acceptance, not gallery screenshots alone, decides whether a workflow presentation is accepted or certified.
