# Classic scenario execution boundary

## Purpose

Own the source-backed Castle opcode adapter from preserved instruction identity through typed domain operation and VM control-flow transition.

## Ownership

- `ClassicOpcodeCatalog`, the handler protocol, and duplicate-safe `ClassicOpcodeHandlerRegistry`.
- Classic opcode handlers grouped by encounter, presentation, and world/time concerns.
- `ScenarioClassicControlFlow` and `ClassicControlFlowOperations` for frame-safe branches, GOSUB, encounters, and timeline completion.
- Domain handler groups for control flow, world/time, character, inventory/economy, encounters, combat/rewards, and presentation-producing operations.
- Source-backed Classic banking, battle, character, combat, inventory, reward, service, and thief operations under `operations/`.
- `ClassicRewardState` owns the bounded saveable progress of a Classic Treasure, battle reward, level, and spell-selection sequence.

## Local Contracts

- `ScenarioRuntimeHandoff.caller_matches_source` is the public codec invariant used after decoding; handoff factories and codecs do not reach through another instance's private helpers.
- Each Classic opcode has one owner. Registration rejects duplicate opcode IDs and VM-owned control-flow opcodes.
- `RealmzRuntimeApi` may coordinate typed waits and resumes but must delegate source-backed domain mutation to a handler or shared game workflow/rule operation.
- `ClassicServiceOperations` owns Shop and Temple opcode execution, request projection, and continuation mutation. `ClassicBankingOperations` owns the Bank request and wealth transfers; both share `ClassicMoneyTransferSupport` for movement-safe money mutation. `ClassicBattleRewardOperations` is the opcode-facing facade over `ClassicBattleLifecycleOperations` and `ClassicRewardOperations`. `ClassicBattleLifecycleOperations` owns battle start, resume, retreat, macro, age, and terminal transitions; `CombatInteractionRequestBuilder` owns the read-only projection of an active battle into its typed command request. Within reward handling, `ClassicBattleRewardBuilder` assembles terminal battle rewards, `ClassicRewardWorkflow` owns Treasure distribution and progression, and `ClassicRewardOperationsSupport` owns their shared transaction policy. Runtime API wrappers expose those operations to `GameSession` without duplicating behavior.
- Classic opcode 6 and opcode 73 load and retain the referenced shop plus their unrestricted or authored acceptance ranges before interpreting the sign: a nonnegative shop identity completes with contextual Shop availability and Castle's application sound 30005 in that order, silencing any active movement effect before the Shop cue; a negative identity immediately enters the same typed shop and leaves it contextually available until ordinary movement clears location services. Opcode 32 similarly configures contextual Temple availability without opening the temple workspace.
- Shop projection preserves Castle's five fixed native stock bands by source slot: Weapons 0–199, Armor 200–399, limb armor 400–599, Magic 600–799, and Supplies 800–999. It projects player-knowable description, weight, fact, portrait, and load data through the shared item view rather than presentation inference. A sale increments the first active matching item or claims the first empty slot in its Classic identity band; that assignment and quantity are save-owned and immutable package stock remains unchanged. A source item without a positive CICN omits its optional projected icon fields rather than producing an invalid typed interaction.
- Classic opcode 19 reads Extra Code slots zero and one as the signed inclusive low/high message IDs passed to Castle's `randrange`; zero displays nothing, a negative selected identity publishes nonblocking narrative by absolute identity, and a positive selection holds the VM at the ordinary saveable Classic textbox acknowledgment before later code or a repeating Encounter may proceed. Equal or descending endpoints retain Castle's one-draw degenerate result instead of becoming validation failures.
- Classic opcode 15 validates any optional message before mutation, then processes each target in stable party order as source sound, one Classic range draw, health mutation, and exact application CICN 12112–12119 effect publication. Opcodes 17/18 publish each resolved target's spell result sound and eight-frame spell-look CICN before its result event; opcode 43 publishes its authored sound and the source default eight-frame effect per stable target. Opcodes 82/83 publish their exact application warning or restoration sound through the shared Classic notification request. Classic opcode 102 forces each selected character across exactly the current Caste victory threshold before applying one level, leaving Castle's post-level balance instead of a synthetic positive point that could retrigger on the next battle reward.
- The character handler owns opcode 89's exact monster lookup and the single documented Tutorial record correction from unavailable 149 to Goblin Shaman 116. The correction applies only after exact lookup fails and never changes another campaign's unresolved-target failure.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 37's validated signed heading and dungeon-view policy at the same teleport transaction. The presentation handler owns opcodes 96/97 policy mutation and publishes only detached policy-change events; neither handler owns a current camera mode.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 68's corrected authoring contract: Calculate reads the second Extra Code slot, multiplies current fatigue before division by 100 with a wide intermediate, truncates toward zero, clamps to 4 through 135, and publishes the ordinary fatigue-change event from committed party state.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 61's fixed or independently signed random X/Y shift and exact four-draw random order. Land shifts reproduce Castle's subsequent `centerpict` boundary collapse against the authoritative map dimensions; the committed event distinguishes the requested delta from the clamped displacement, while an invalid dungeon destination remains an explicit safe-runtime failure rather than unchecked field access.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 12's exact signed tile write. Its save-owned terrain value drives both normalized land/battle terrain and negative special-land presentation; package topology remains immutable.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 13's map-record percentage mutation. Providence preserves every authored placed Action Point reachable through that mutation; references to unplaced native rows remain Castle-compatible silent no-ops rather than runtime failures.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 95's fixed or one-draw random dungeon-heading mutation because heading changes subsequent simulation movement, not merely camera presentation.
- Land opcode 101 reverses the last committed step and finishes the complete VM timeline. Its `party_backed_up` event lets the post-move owner skip AP removal, header relocation, and further trigger processing. In dungeons it remains a no-op that continues the issuing code.
- Opcode 56 loss target `-1` resumes only after the ordinary Party Death hook revives the defeated party. It preserves Castle's two application warnings and interstitial sound, subtracts `2,000 * level` experience from every party member, reverses the last committed land step, publishes the shared backup event, and finishes the issuing VM timeline. A missing or invalid land backup direction fails before mutation; dungeons retain Castle's no-movement result.
- The presentation handler owns opcode 62 as a blocking, saveable `classic-scrolling-text` acknowledgement carrying the operand's exact signed scenario `TEXT` resource identity. It never interprets that operand as a Data SD2 message ID or copies resource bytes into simulation. It publishes the detached scrolling-text event, then resumes the following opcode exactly once only after the typed acknowledgement; presentation timing and scrolling never enter simulation or consume RNG.
- The control-flow handler owns opcode 59's source-defective faced-tile normalization and unconditional typed branch. The presentation handler owns save mutation and detached refresh events for opcode 71's X/Y display flag and opcodes 93/94's compass flag; redundant compass transitions alone publish warning text.
- The control-flow handler validates opcode 86's test kind and race descriptor before evaluating its selected-or-complete party predicate. Its matcher may inspect immutable character and content facts only; branch construction, event publication, and destination transfer remain in the opcode operation.
- The world/time handler owns opcode 57's offscreen appearance override, opcode 70's exact position bookmark/restore, and opcode 92's offscreen random-region chance/geometry mutation. Opcode 92 safely reproduces Castle's PC fallback by normalizing a negative map to map zero and any rectangle index outside 0 through 19 to fixed slot zero. The control-flow handler owns opcode 78's faced-cell semantic branch. All four mutate or query authoritative save-owned world state, consume no RNG, and publish projection invalidation only after successful mutation.
- The world/time handler emits opcode 20's destination-trigger handoff and finishes the issuing VM timeline only when the authoritative destination cell carries a placed Action Point identity. An unmarked destination completes the teleport without a VM directive so later authored slots continue; opcode 45 never emits that handoff.
- The control-flow owner treats obsolete registration opcodes 84 and 98 as explicit open-source pass-through markers; package readiness never mistakes an active registration row for an unknown or silent no-op.
- The battle facade may project the current typed combat request for an active direct-session battle. This read-only projection shares command legality with scenario-owned combat but does not create a VM wait or continuation.
- Combat request projection supplies every positioned party or monster combatant's read-only inspection record. Character entries use player-knowable inventory views and resolved weapon damage; monster entries use source attack definitions and may expose visible equipped gear, but never authored loot identities.
- The battle facade may accept a debug-completed victory only against the exact base Classic or Safe combat continuation whose battle is now terminal. It delegates immediately to ordinary ally selection and reward construction; no debug-only reward or VM-return path exists.
- Opcode 100 may run only through the active battle-macro runtime. It delegates forced victory to `CombatFlow`, terminates the nested macro immediately, routes through ordinary ally and reward continuations in experience-only mode, then converts the save-owned slot-eight combat sentinel into `finish-timeline` only after the terminal reward commits.
- Opcode 124 builds each source monster through the session RNG, then places its complete footprint with the shared Castle expanding battlefield scan before adding it to combat and turn order. Battle-round macros center on source-order monster zero and retain the authored monster side; combatant-owned macro contexts center on and inherit the positioned source unless Extra Code supplies an explicit side.
- Opcode 17 in a combatant-owned macro delegates non-damaging area and side-group condition spells to the game macro-spell resolver. The retained source footprint supplies the center; the active actor supplies Castle's resistance caster. This path needs no picked characters, spends no resources, and never advances the interrupted activation. Unsupported macro spell families fail explicitly; ordinary picked-character opcode 17 and whole-party opcode 18 retain their existing paths.
- `ClassicThiefEncounterOperations` owns the dedicated eight-action thief workspace, mutable encounter flags, damage-before-spell trap ordering, stable selected/all-party trap targets, timed Pick Lock handoff, signed click/non-click result text, authored result routing, and the matching typed continuations. Trap spells delegate to the game scenario-group resolver and spend no caster resource; this owner must not flatten those actions into Complex Encounter buttons or replace Castle's trained-ability 5-through-12 mapping with host-authored rolls.
- Thief success/failure text zero means no message, even when the scenario contains Data SD2 record zero. Preserve the independent sound, reward, and owner-bound result continuation; positive and negative nonzero text retain their existing click semantics.
- Simple, Complex, and Thief prompt identity zero means no authored prompt. Never look up Data SD2 record zero for these encounter headers; preserve nonzero lookup, available responses, and the issuing continuation.
- Opcode 35 eliminates its one-based Simple option and immediately requests the remaining choices through a `reopenResult` continuation. Resume replaces the active result without incrementing its iteration, rerunning the issuing encounter opcode, emitting opening sound 20005, or executing later abandoned slots. Retain GOSUB ownership when replacing a program inside a call; neither option elimination nor result replacement pops Castle's stack.
- Complex Encounter catalogs project only living exact owners. Spell continuation validates that the selected owner knows the selected packed spell identity; item continuation validates the exact owner, carried instance, and packed item identity. Presentation-visible item names, icons, charges, and equipped state are projected by the request owner and never reconstructed from hidden package facts.
- Simple, Complex, and Thief result programs retain the issuing encounter frame until the authored `maxTimes` selection count is exhausted. Opening a Simple or Complex Encounter requests Castle application sound 20005 as an explicitly Reduced Sound-eligible workspace cue. Complex Action requests publish Castle's required toggle count without revealing the successful group; their response carries the complete selected slot set, exact group equality selects the authored Action result, and a same-sized mismatch selects fallback result four. The current attempt is typed save state; eliminated Simple options survive each repeat, Back exits immediately, and a final Complex fallback result four becomes Castle's timeout result three when `maxTimes` exceeds one. Opcode 24's typed directive terminates the complete timeline from any nested encounter-result transfer without changing the issuing AP's persistent program or an explicit opcode-7 replacement; opcode 34's distinct directive unwinds to the matching rewound opcode 4/5 source and resumes its following slot with encounter-only context cleared.
- Opcodes 67, 72, 75, and 85 may enter a Simple or Complex Encounter without fabricating an opcode 4/5 action. `ENTER_ENCOUNTER` creates a serializable encounter-origin frame that retains the already-selected identity and signed GOSUB state; repeating results return to that frame, final results close it, and opcode 34 unwinds it before resuming a retained caller. Opcode 85's RNG draw belongs only to the issuing control-flow handler and is never replayed by encounter continuation.
- Opcode 44 is valid only in a Complex Encounter result and persists its one-based result operand as the current encounter's eliminated zero-based result. Opcode 125 owns a weak-runtime, serializable nested death-macro continuation; interaction and restore resume the exact macro and remaining stable monster sequence before the issuing scenario instruction continues.
- Simple and Complex Back finish the complete issuing VM timeline, including nested result/repeat and signed GOSUB frames. Castle's direct cancellation return is not opcode 111: no enclosing encounter or later caller slot resumes. The session alone reverses the entered land step and preserves its AP without another clock or RNG transaction.
- Classic Choice mode zero finishes the current VM timeline and publishes a typed backout request for its session owner. Mode four finishes the timeline without inventing encounter-option mutation. The runtime never directly moves the party or finalizes the issuing AP.
- Terminal reward ownership lasts through every source-ordered reward workspace. Initial construction and each resumed reward operation checkpoint canonical game state and gameplay RNG; failures restore both, discard speculative events, and leave the owning continuation retryable. Persisted summoned actors are excluded before content validation and consume no reward draws. Ordinary and opcode-2 mode-5 victories otherwise share the exact money-then-incidental draw sequence; mode 5 suppresses authored monster wealth/items but not experience, recovered fumbles, or eligible Classic items 806/877. `FD-REWARD-001` repeats one queued positive recipient through separate saveable level-result acknowledgements until its carried victory points are nonpositive, then queues that recipient for spell selection at most once; an invalid nonpositive threshold publishes its correction event, normalizes the unusable positive balance, and grants no level. Opcode-2 mode 10 bypasses allies, fumbles, ordinary rewards, and Party Death: victory replaces scenario wealth with escrowed wealth, restores escrowed equipment, and retains scenario-carried items in party storage; total defeat revives every character to one stamina, clears Animated, retains escrow, consumes no RNG, and returns `RESTART_CURRENT_PROGRAM`. The VM applies that typed directive only to the issuing program frame. Battle Discover Magic carries aligned monster-loot marks into reward-local item-instance IDs, persists them through the reward continuation, and exposes the magic presentation only for definitions that are actually magical; assignment or discard removes the local mark rather than adding an inventory fact. Treasure lore projection keeps Detect Magic and Identify Objects as stable sibling controls, disables an already completed method with an explicit reason, and independently recomputes the other method's eligible casters from authoritative remaining spell points. Level-up spell selection projects each candidate's application-owned description with its identity, cost, and selected state. Opcode 48 enters battle with only the stable selected party IDs, so initiative, battlefield membership, terminal detection, and rewards exclude the party members who stayed behind. If every selected participant falls, it requests Castle warning sound 6000 with `There is nobody left to collect any treasure.`, skips Party Death, clears combat, and returns the untouched remainder to exploration. Its optional fixed treasure is a separately persisted second stage after victory; combat cleanup, the after-message, and VM return occur only after that stage completes.
- Nested battle and death-macro VMs call the owning runtime API through a weak reference so the session graph has no `RefCounted` ownership cycle.
- Classic control-flow results cross the runtime/VM boundary as `ScenarioVmDirective` variants. Raw directive dictionaries are codec data only and may not be inspected or constructed by handlers.
- A Classic result-program branch may carry a validated entry cursor through `ScenarioVmDirective`; the VM applies it when constructing the target frame, and the strict codec preserves zero as the backward-compatible default. Control-flow and inventory handlers derive Simple or Complex result program identities only from the closed active encounter context. Opcode 38 preserves Castle's direct zero-based encounter-result index and source cursor while branching within the active Simple or Complex result table.
- Opcode 33 derives gold versus gems from the sign of its authored amount. Insufficient pooled-plus-carried funds leave state unchanged and request Castle warning 50 with application sound 6000. XAP mode zero evaluates its failure, success, or unconditional predicate after that payment attempt and replaces execution without GOSUB, including exact XAP zero. This does not certify its separate encounter-result, slot-seven, or Keep Codes modes.
- Opcode 52 preserves the incoming tracked identities for Picked Only and validates exact one-based positions before replacing the selection. Opcode 55 selector zero tests any pick, selectors 1 through 6 test their one-based party positions under `FD-SCENARIO-003`, negative selectors test an at-least count, and unsupported positive selectors safely follow the authored failure path. Opcode 81 uses its documented whole-party, nonempty-picked, and one-based-position sets under `FD-SCENARIO-004`; it never performs Castle's shifted or out-of-bounds read.
- Live execution context crosses the runtime/VM boundary only as `ScenarioExecutionContext`. `ScenarioExecutionContextCodec` is the only public dictionary boundary; unknown provenance fields fail restoration, while ordinary copies remain typed and do not round-trip through serialization.
- Live continuation queues contain typed interaction bodies. Dictionary payloads may enter only from detached domain events and are decoded once before the continuation is constructed.
- Unknown opcodes fail explicitly. There is no script-name dispatch or GDScript fallback.
- Handlers are explicitly constructed, retain only session-owned pure dependencies, and never access Nodes or host services.
