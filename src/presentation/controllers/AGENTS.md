# Presentation workspace controller contract

## Purpose

Own route-local presentation state and control construction behind typed detached views.

## Local Contracts

- Controllers receive an explicit target container and detached values; they never receive `GameSession`, repositories, or the owning router.
- Controllers emit typed intents, host actions, or presentation-setting changes. They never mutate gameplay state.
- `ClassicScreenRouter` alone mounts primary workspaces and restores route focus.
- `ClassicWorkspacePresenter` owns route-local controller composition, detached route state, content rendering, and route-specific audio. The router supplies the mounted route body and navigation-owned back label; it does not render domain content.
- A controller may preserve selection, filtering, sorting, tabs, and draft text for its own route only.
- Application-route controllers own compact task-specific character selection inside their routed workspace. They do not depend on roster-row interaction, and item-local operations such as Trade keep their exact source item and recipient choices together.
- `InventoryWorkspaceController` owns Castle's paper-white, icon-led carried-item ledger, compact selected-character command rail, integrated six-member portrait selector, and full-width selected-item record. The ledger uses Black Chancery item names, dark ink, a pale-gold selection, and only detached identified state in its blue/red annotations. Inventory actions retain their stable command identities but render as uniform Rebuilt raised/inset slate controls instead of importing Castle's inconsistent word-bearing button slabs. The controller may reflow the record vertically in the optional Classic profile, but it keeps exact-instance actions and Trade staging together and never calculates character or item facts.
- `ServicesWorkspaceController` owns the ordinary Party Wealth route, not a catalog of hypothetical services. Wide composition separates the detached party pool, six adventurer wealth records, and selected-character Swap controls; Classic composition condenses character selection into the Swap pane. Pool, Share, denomination increments, load facts, and disabled reasons remain core-owned, while Done and character selection are presentation-owned.
- `SpellsWorkspaceController` is composed for the narrow right-side workspace that exactly replaces the Party roster while the map and exploration footer remain visible. Its compact caster selector, Classic level/list/record hierarchy, legal power rail, and fixed Cast/Back actions must remain reachable at both supported profiles; Fast Spell and scroll-case tabs retain their typed intent paths. The controller may format supplied scaling, target, resistance, save, cost, and availability facts but cannot infer legal powers, targets, effects, or resource identities.
- `CreatureLibraryWorkspaceController` owns read-only selection and rendering for current held-over allies. It consumes detached `MonsterView` records and exact media keys; it never constructs combat state, changes ally membership, or treats current allies as the Bestiary denominator. Wide composition uses one backed current-allies list beside a dominant record, while the optional Classic profile stacks those same panes so facts remain legible. List and detail art must resolve the ally's exact CICN resource key with nearest-neighbor sampling; unavailable media stays visibly absent rather than substituting a generic creature.
- `MapsJournalWorkspaceController` owns three route-local workspaces: saved/current location notes, acquired player maps, and authored journal records. The route screen is the sole heading owner; the controller adds only counts, selectors, and bounded record previews. It preserves tab and selected-record state, emits only the typed current-location note intent, and renders player maps through `PlayerMapPresenter`; it never acquires a map, edits authored journal text, or invents thumbnails for missing media.
- `SystemWorkspaceController` exposes the host-owned traveled-area preview and Auto Note toggles in Controls. Both persist only through `PresentationSettings`; the former changes map rendering and the latter selects the acknowledgement payload, without mutating simulation directly. Its Audio tab keeps master, effects, and music volumes separate, persists global music enablement, and opens the shell-owned playlist modal without owning playback.
- `SystemWorkspaceController` keeps Save & Load separate from Display, Audio, Accessibility, Controls, and Diagnostics. The route screen is the sole title owner; the controller adds a bounded campaign/rules context line and reflows setting rows from aligned wide records to compact vertical records through `UiLayoutProfile`. The save workspace owns slot/source selection, one detached preview, and fixed host actions; a host-requested Save and Quit mode replaces Quick Save with one selected-slot Save and Quit action, while Back cancels that host mode. It distinguishes primary and backup loads and leaves corrupt/incompatible records visible with their exact reason. Preference tabs emit only existing typed presentation-setting changes and apply immediately; they never alter Classic rules or invent Apply/Reset, deletion, screenshots, save mutation, or migration controls absent from the host contracts.
- `CampaignLibraryController` owns the Black Chancery front-door composition, licensed looping application animation inside its scalable app-owned ornamental frame, fast manifest/readiness rows, selected `CampaignSummaryView` record, and one fixed package-operation host. It may resolve only the selected summary's exact `splashAssetId`; it cannot decode package content during discovery, classify free-form readiness errors, or retain terminal host failures absent from its detached operation view. External installation starts from one host file picker filtered to `.realmz2`; no developer path field appears in the ordinary library. Package progress disables competing install/refresh/scenario actions without displacing party controls. `CampaignPartySetupController` composes explicit shared setup state with independent inspection, party-assembly, and five-step creation controllers; those collaborators never inherit behavior from one another or receive the router. The facade preserves construction, visibility, focus, and its public signal/method boundary. Both public controllers mount under an explicit overlay host supplied by the shell scene.
- `PartySetupCharacterCreationController` owns the full-stage five-step local draft surface. Identity, Race/Caste, Appearance, Review, and Spells may retain only presentation-local selections before emitting typed intents. Race drives the left/right selector relationship; each Race and Caste record pairs a narrow pressed selector with a wider detached detail, named-compatibility pane, and source-backed rule facts. Empty source descriptions occupy no UI and never become invented fallback prose. The controller may suppress only literal numbered fallback labels and may sort compatible Castes before incompatible records, but it cannot infer compiler provenance or calculate modifiers. Appearance exposes exact package portrait/tactical catalogs as paged two-row, six-column grids labelled from explicit `recommended_race_ids`; it never infers grouping from labels or numeric ranges. Portrait selection may follow the proven Classic resource pairing until the player independently overrides the tactical icon. Review consumes the detached provisional `CharacterView` through the shared complete character sheet; it may resolve that view's exact appearance and item media, but cannot calculate rules, invent restrictions, or substitute resource identities. Starting Spells shares `ClassicSpellSelectionChrome` with the ordinary spellbook, keeps only presentation-local level/focus state, and emits the complete typed selected-ID set so choices on hidden levels survive filtering. Pressed state communicates selection without modifier-key helper copy. `CharacterSpellOptionView` supplies the exact Classic resolution-effect resource sequence; the creator may animate only those IDs through scenario-first application-media resolution and must not derive art from a numeric spell ID. Actions remain outside the scrolling record body.

## Verification

- Exercise controllers through their public `present` and signal boundaries.
- Route lifecycle tests own primary-workspace exclusivity and modal input ownership; do not add one test per overlap symptom.
